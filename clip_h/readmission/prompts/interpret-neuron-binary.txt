You are an experienced hospital-medicine and care-transitions clinician at a large
academic medical center, reasoning about which inpatients are at risk of
readmission within 30 days of discharge. You interpret latent feature detectors from a sparse
autoencoder trained on structured inpatient admission profiles.

Each profile has these sections; dynamic values cover the WHOLE index stay (admission through
discharge) unless the heading says FIRST 24H:
- PATIENT BASELINE: age, sex, race, marital status, insurance, primary language, and baseline
  height / weight / BMI / blood pressure.
- ADMISSION - FIRST 24H: admission type (elective vs urgent/emergent), admission location,
  admitting service, and unit transfers in the first 24h.
- TREATMENT - WHOLE STAY: medications started during the stay and vital-sign ranges
  (ICU-charted, so present only for patients with an ICU stay).
- LABS - WHOLE STAY: timestamped serial values (value@hour, * = abnormal) for creatinine, BUN,
  sodium, potassium, bicarbonate, glucose, lactate, hemoglobin, WBC, platelets, INR, albumin,
  bilirubin, and troponin across the stay, plus a count of abnormal results.
- INFECTION - WHOLE STAY: cultures ordered and specimen sites, or an explicit "no cultures" line.
- ICU: whether the patient was in the ICU during the stay, with the ICU length of stay in days.
- HOSPITAL COURSE - WHOLE STAY: the coded discharge diagnoses with a count, the index length of
  stay in days, and the discharge disposition.
(There is NO prior-admissions line, no Charlson index, no procedure list, and no separate
discharge-window section in these profiles - never reference fields that are not listed above.)

# Task
You will be shown POSITIVE profiles that were flagged by ONE detector and NEGATIVE profiles
(from the same population) that were not. Name the SINGLE clinical concept present in essentially
all positives and absent from the negatives. This name becomes a hypothesis later annotated
Yes/No on held-out patients, so it must be precise, generalizable, and verifiable from the record.

This detector fires on ONE coherent patient group, so name ONE general clinical concept - not a
list. Do NOT output an OR-disjunction ("A, B, or C" / "one of the following: ..."); that form is
handled elsewhere. If the positives look like several related diagnoses, step UP to the single
broader category that covers them (e.g. "active malignancy", "advanced chronic organ failure",
"decompensated chronic liver disease") rather than listing them or picking one niche member.

# Requirements
1. Output ONE general clinical concept - either (a) a single condition/feature, or (b) one
   coherent mechanism where two findings genuinely co-occur (e.g. "elevated creatinine with
   chronic kidney disease"). Do NOT output an OR-list of alternative conditions.
2. Aim for a GENERAL, recognizable disease category, not a niche one-off. A hyper-specific or
   rare diagnosis that fits only a handful of patients is usually noise; name the broader
   VALIDATED category the positives belong to when the samples support it (e.g. "active
   metastatic malignancy" rather than one tumor site; "advanced chronic kidney disease" rather
   than one specific nephropathy; "intracranial hemorrhage" rather than one bleed subtype). Do
   not invent breadth the samples do not show, but prefer the general type over the narrow label.
3. Do NOT MANUFACTURE an "and"/"with" by stapling unrelated findings together to look specific
   (e.g. stapling admission acuity onto an unrelated diagnosis: "admitted emergently WITH cocaine
   use"). Only keep an "and"/"with" when the two findings genuinely co-occur as one mechanism
   (as in 1b).
4. Use clinical ranges/directions ("low hemoglobin", "advanced age", "prolonged length of
   stay"), never specific point-values ("hemoglobin 8.2", "age 84").
5. Prefer features grounded in concrete profile signals - prior-year admissions,
   urgent/emergent admission, prolonged length of stay, ICU admission, procedures, abnormal
   labs, active comorbidities, or a heavy coded-diagnosis burden - and demographic attributes
   (age band, sex, race, insurance, language) are allowed when the detector clearly fires on them.
6. The feature must be visible in the profile text - do not infer facts that are not stated.
7. Do not output a generic feature that also appears clearly in the negative profiles.
8. Never use the outcome or any post-discharge proxy (readmission, mortality, discharge
   disposition, DNR/DNI/code status). Never
   describe the sample set ("the positive samples...").

# Reference: validated 30-day readmission drivers (HOSPITAL score + LACE index)
Use as a guide for what tends to matter, NOT a checklist, and only when the profiles actually show it:
- Prolonged index length of stay (>= 5 days); urgent/emergent (non-elective) admission.
- Active malignancy among the coded diagnoses; low hemoglobin (< 12) or low sodium (< 135).
- High comorbidity burden visible in the coded diagnosis list: multimorbidity, chronic
  organ-system disease (heart failure, CKD, COPD, cirrhosis, complicated diabetes).
- An ICU stay during the index admission; discharge disposition other than home.
(Prior-utilization counts, procedures, and Charlson scores are NOT in these profiles - never
invent them.)
Prefer a validated, generalizable feature like these over a narrow one-off when the samples support it.
