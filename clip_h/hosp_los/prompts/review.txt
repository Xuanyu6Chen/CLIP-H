<reference_knowledge>
Use these validated drivers of a prolonged length of stay both as a sanity check on plausibility
and on expected_direction. Do NOT raise a score just because a hypothesis resembles a driver.
- High acuity / illness severity: ICU admission, mechanical ventilation, vasopressors, dialysis.
- Sepsis or serious infection; shock or unstable first-24h vitals; oliguria / acute kidney injury.
- Major or complex surgery; multimorbidity and high comorbidity burden.
- Urgent/emergent (non-elective) admission; markedly abnormal first-24h labs (low hemoglobin,
  elevated lactate, deranged renal function).
- Advanced age and frailty markers.
General direction: features marking higher acuity, organ dysfunction, or surgical complexity
predict a LONGER stay; a clean elective low-acuity presentation predicts a SHORTER stay.
</reference_knowledge>

<leakage_and_scope>
The OUTCOME is the length of stay itself. Any hypothesis that references the length of stay,
discharge timing or disposition, in-hospital death, or ANY event after the first 24 hours is
LEAKAGE and must be scored to drop (leakage_risk 2). This is the single most important check:
"patient had a long / prolonged hospital stay", "patient was discharged to a facility", and
"patient died in hospital" are all LEAKAGE, not valid hypotheses - never keep or propose them.

Cohort scope: this is an all-admissions length-of-stay cohort (no psychiatric / obstetric / rehab
exclusions), so a hypothesis is NOT out of scope merely for being psychiatric, obstetric, or
rehabilitation-related - judge it on clinical validity and leakage like any other candidate.
</leakage_and_scope>

<grouped_hypotheses>
Some candidates are intentionally DISJUNCTIVE: one hypothesis naming several conditions joined by
OR - sometimes along one clinical axis, sometimes a pooled set of rare serious conditions across
different systems - e.g. "Patient has decompensated chronic liver disease: SBP, cirrhosis with
portal hypertension, or hepatorenal syndrome". These come from detectors that fire on a
heterogeneous but coherent group of patients, so a disjunction is a faithful description, NOT
vagueness. Judge each disjunction as ONE hypothesis:
- clinical_validity is HIGH when every listed condition is a serious, length-of-stay-relevant
  condition. Members do NOT need to share one organ system: a pooled group of rare serious
  conditions across systems (e.g. "one of the following: active leukemia, septic shock, or
  respiratory failure") is valid and expected for low-prevalence detectors.
- clinical_validity is LOW when the group mixes serious stay-prolonging conditions with trivial,
  protective, or neutral ones, lists generic/common attributes, or is clinically nonsensical.
Do NOT collapse a coherent disjunction into a single condition, and do NOT split it into its
members. You MAY also MERGE several separate candidates that are each serious,
length-of-stay-relevant conditions into a single OR-disjunction, giving the merged entry all their

system. Join with OR only, never "and"/"with"; never merge in trivial, protective, or neutral
conditions. Every merged group is re-annotated and scored downstream, so propose only clinically
sensible groups.
</grouped_hypotheses>
